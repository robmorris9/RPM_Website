Operating thesis

Finding structure in ambiguous business problems

I'm an operator who looks for structure in ambiguous problems. Across 14 years at Citi and KeyBank, I have led teams close to the transaction, worked on markets and balance-sheet decisions, and built portfolio processes where data, judgment, controls, and execution have to stay connected.

The common thread is not a bank function or a particular tool. I learn how an organization works, find where a decision needs more structure, and build a clearer way to make and carry it out. Analytics, process design, and automation matter when they make that system faster, more reliable, or easier to govern.

At KeyBank, that currently includes a risk-scoring dashboard in UAT, credit-line risk-rating automation and governance, and early-stage revolving-line-to-term work with Product and Fair Lending. Banking is where I built this operating pattern; it is not the ceiling on where I can apply it.

How I operate

Build the decision and the system around it

I tend to work on problems that need both analysis and implementation. I clarify the business decision, understand how the work and data move, and help define the procedures, controls, ownership, and monitoring around the answer.

  1. 01

    Read the system, not just the metric

    I look at how work moves, where decisions sit, which constraints matter, and how the data was produced before deciding what a number means.

  2. 02

    Design the decision and control together

    A recommendation is incomplete until ownership, exceptions, procedures, monitoring, and escalation are clear.

  3. 03

    Use technology to remove friction

    I use automation when it shortens a decision cycle, improves the information, or removes repetitive work. The goal is better judgment and reliable execution.

Why Director next

I have already been doing core pieces of Director-scope work: coordinating across functions without relying on formal authority, designing decisions and controls together, and turning ambiguity into an executable, governed process. The next step is broader ownership of the systems, priorities, and teams around that work.

Case studies

From messy problem to repeatable system

01

KeyBank · In UAT

A risk-scoring system tied to compensation decisions

Problem
A portfolio risk score can influence relationship-manager compensation, including bonuses. That makes consistency, review, and governance as important as the analysis itself.
My role
I built the dashboard from scratch and am carrying it through UAT, including the operating process around how its output will be reviewed and used.
Thinking
If a score affects people, it cannot be treated as a dashboard-only deliverable. Decision rights, controls, exceptions, and monitoring have to be designed with it.
Action / system
Built the risk-scoring dashboard and worked across stakeholders to define its review and governance path.
Result
The dashboard is in UAT and is designed to inform compensation decisions; production impact is not yet claimed.
Why it matters
It shows how I connect analytics to a decision system when the organizational stakes are real.
02

KeyBank · Active initiative

Credit-line risk-rating automation and governance

Problem
Risk ratings on credit cards and revolving lines need to change as customer relationships evolve. The action has to be timely and consistent without weakening control.
My role
I am designing the automation and governance for freezing or downgrading those ratings and helping turn it into an ongoing operating process.
Thinking
The decision and the control are one system: logic, ownership, exceptions, procedures, and monitoring all matter.
Action / system
Working through the decision rules, ownership, exception handling, procedures, and monitoring needed to move the work from analysis into operation.
Result
The initiative is creating a repeatable mechanism for managing rating changes over the life of a relationship; no final outcome metric is claimed yet.
Why it matters
It demonstrates governed automation: more consistent action without losing accountability.
03

KeyBank · Completed

Regulation B adverse-action automation

Problem
A manual, Excel-based Regulation B adverse-action process was handling hundreds of applications a day. The risk was structural: a high-volume regulated process depended on repeated manual steps.
My role
I identified the process risk and fully automated it end to end.
Thinking
At this volume, the answer was not more manual effort. It was to remove repetitive handling while preserving monitoring.
Action / system
Replaced the Excel workflow with an automated process and retained risk-partner monitoring as the control layer.
Result
The process now runs automatically at current volume, with outputs monitored by risk partners.
Why it matters
It is a practical example of using automation to strengthen both efficiency and control.
04

Citi · Completed

Direct SWIFT settlement migration

Problem
A client's settlement setup relied on a third party, creating avoidable network costs that were not obvious at the headline-process level.
My role
I flagged the opportunity through a product-processor-level review, then drove the sustained work of moving the client to direct SWIFT settlement.
Thinking
The opportunity appeared only when I looked below the aggregate process and traced how that client's product was actually being processed.
Action / system
Worked through the client and operational migration required to remove the third party and establish direct SWIFT settlement.
Result
Approximately $29M in annual network settlement savings.
Why it matters
It shows a pattern I still use: find the overlooked structural detail, then stay with the work through implementation.
05

Citi · Completed team effort

Balance-sheet response during the 2023 crisis

Problem
During the 2023 regional-banking crisis, Citi Treasury needed entity-by-entity action to improve liquidity positioning and keep more funding within US entities despite Citi's limited standalone US presence.
My role
I was a core, hands-on contributor, not the sole owner of the $10B result, working with international Treasury teams including Dubai and Hong Kong.
Thinking
The answer could not be a single aggregate move. Deposits and HQLA had to be repositioned within each entity's constraints and the cross-border structure.
Action / system
Worked entity by entity to analyze and execute the required deposit and HQLA repositioning.
Result
The team optimized roughly $10B of the balance sheet, enabling roughly $5B to remain within Citi's US entities.
Why it matters
It shows how I contribute in a high-stakes system: across boundaries, under time pressure, without losing precision.
